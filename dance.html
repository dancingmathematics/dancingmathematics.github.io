Practice

Balinese dance

Since 2021 I have performed at more than thirty public events across Northern California, primarily with Gamelan Sekar Jaya, and on several occasions at the invitation of the Consulate General of the Republic of Indonesia in San Francisco.

Why this is on a research website

Balinese dance is a discipline of exact spatial relations. Agem requires the body to hold a specific configuration of angles. Tapak Sirang Pada asks the feet to form ninety degrees. Travelling across a stage means maintaining a relation to a reference you cannot see and no one has drawn for you.

Dancers do this by constructing lines — between the shoulder and the far edge of the stage, between one foot and a fixed point behind, between the body and the audience. These lines are imagined, held briefly, and discarded. Nobody teaches them explicitly and nobody writes them down.

They are also, structurally, auxiliary lines: the same objects that make geometry problems solvable. Recognising that correspondence is what the GRiD project is built on. So the performance record below is not a hobby appended to a CV. It is the empirical ground the research rests on, and I keep it with the same care.
