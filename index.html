Ratih Ayu Apsari, Ph.D.

Research Associate, Embodied Design Research Laboratory

University of California, Berkeley

I am a learning scientist and mathematics teacher educator with ten years of university-level teaching experience. My research promotes epistemic diversity and cultural funds of knowledge by integrating culturally situated embodied cognition into mathematics education. I am currently working on Geometry Resources in Dance (GRiD), a gridded floor mat that objectifies the tacit attentional anchors dancers use for movement coordination into auxiliary lines for geometric reasoning.

My research agenda is grounded in my transdisciplinary training in mathematics education and traditional Balinese dance. Beyond research, I continue to support non-profit educational communities, serve as journal reviewers, and mentor undergraduate and pre-doctoral researchers. Whenever the opportunity arises, I enjoy practicing and performing Balinese dance.

What I work on

Three threads

Geometry Resources in Dance

A low-cost gridded floor mat that turns the tacit attentional anchors of Balinese dance into explicit auxiliary lines for geometric reasoning. Studied with fifth-graders in Bali and California.

Project page →

Collaborative embodied cognition

How two learners who literally see a shared mathematical object differently break down, notice, and reconcile that difference — traced through multimodal interaction analysis and mobile eye tracking.

Read more →

Epistemic diversity in education

Cultural funds of knowledge, transnational teacher educators, and what it takes for educational design to take non-Western ways of knowing seriously rather than decoratively.
